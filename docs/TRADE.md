# Trade and Treachery

The second game mode, chosen by the host in the lobby. It is inspired by Christian Aid's
Trading Game: a simple picture of how trade can widen or narrow the gap between rich and poor
countries. All wording is our own.

## The idea

Every player is one country. Countries turn raw materials into goods and sell the goods to
the market. They start unequal, and nobody is told so. Everything else is left to the
players: what to trade, at what price, and whether to keep their word. Three to five people,
no bots, one live session of 10, 15 or 20 minutes.

## From the paper game to this one

| Paper game | Here |
| --- | --- |

| Scissors | Workshops (one job at a time each) |
| Ruler, compass, set-square, protractor | Sawmill, forge, loom, mill, power plant |
| Five shapes worth £150 to £500 | Planks, cloth, tools, food, electricity, furniture, machinery (40 to 700 gold) |
| The banker, batches of five | The market, which is not a player; it buys goods in batches of five |
| Grade A, B and C envelopes | Three kinds of start, dealt at random |
| The leader's interventions | Timed events |
| The gummed paper | Amber |

## The three starts

Starts are dealt at random, and the details change every game, so no two countries (and no
two games) are alike.

| | Gold | Industry | Raw materials |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Industrial country | 600 | 2 workshops and three of the five other industries | 1 or 2 each of three raw materials |
| Raw-material country | 200 | no workshop; half the time one industry it cannot yet use | 12 to 16 each of three raw materials, 2 of a fourth, 2 amber |
| Poor country | 200 | no workshop; often one industry it cannot yet use | 4 to 6 each of two raw materials, 2 of a third |

Three players get one of each; four get a second raw-material country; five also get a second
poor country. Nobody starts able to make everything, so the industrial country needs
buildings as well as materials from the others. 

## What can be made

## What you can do

- **Make goods.** Each product needs a workshop plus its own building, uses raw materials,
  and takes 5 to 12 seconds an item.
- **Sell to the market** in batches of five, at the market's prices. The market will also buy
  raw materials, but only for 25 gold each.
- **Build:** a workshop costs 900 gold, a mill 400, the other industries 600, a border guard 200.
- **Buy and sell shares** in four companies (below).
- **Safe swap:** offer anything for anything, to one country or to whoever accepts first. It
  happens all at once or not at all.
- **Send:** give something away with no guarantee. Loans, aid and deals on trust are made
  this way, and nothing stops a promise being broken.
- **Talk:** to everyone, or privately to one country.

- **Lend a building** for 1 to 5 minutes.

## The host's two switches

- **Can you see what other countries hold?** On: everyone's holdings are visible, like tables
  in a room. Off: you see only your own, the news says only that a deal was made, and nobody
  can check what anyone claims. Everything is revealed when the market closes.
- **Lent buildings.** Either they come back by themselves when the loan ends, or they only
  come back if the borrower returns them.

With both switched off, a poor country can win by bluffing: it starts worth only a few
hundred, so talking its way into one workshop it never gives back (worth 900) more than
doubles it, while the industrial country would need to add thousands to keep pace.

## Payday

Every minute, every share pays out part of its current price in gold. The rate is set by the
length of the game so that, over a whole game, a share pays out about twice its price: 20% a
minute in a 10-minute game, about 13% in 15 minutes, 10% in 20. A company in trouble (a
scandal or a crash) pays nothing for a payday or two. That is the only thing
that happens at payday: there is no tax, and nothing has to be fed.

## The market's orders

The market usually buys five of a product at a time. Now and then, for a while, it wants a
product three at a time, or one at a time; the news says so and the sell button changes.

## The stock exchange

The heart of the game, with trade between countries. Four companies: Timber Co, Ironworks,
Farm & Loom, Coal & Power. Each has only **10 shares**. Shares start at 100 gold.

- Buying a share from the company raises its price by 5% for the next buyer; selling one back
  lowers it by 5%. So the first buyer gets the best price, and a big buyer pushes the price up
  for everyone (which also raises what every holder is paid).
- Once all ten are owned, the only way to get one is from another country: in a safe swap,
  as a gift, or on a promise.
- Prices also move every 30 seconds (up or down by as much as a tenth, with a slight pull back
  towards 100) and jump when the news touches their industry.
- **Shares can lose money.** A scandal halves one company's price and stops its next payout;
  a crash takes two thirds off the dearest company and stops two payouts; a panic hits them
  all. A takeover bid, on the other hand, lifts the cheapest company by 60%.
- Shares count towards worth at their current price.

The limit of ten shares per company keeps the payouts from snowballing: shares are a prize to
fight and bargain over rather than a place to park gold.

## Borders

A border guard costs 200 gold. The game warns that raiders are gathering; when they strike,
they hit one country without a guard and take a fifth of its gold and a quarter of its raw
materials.

## Luck

Each game also holds about one piece of luck every two minutes, each falling on a single
country, alternating good and bad. Good luck is likelier to find a poorer country and bad
luck a richer one, but either can strike anyone. The country concerned is told the details;
everyone else only hears that something happened there.

- **Good:** a lost fortune (150 gold), a rich foreign buyer (the next batch sold to the market
  pays double), skilled workers (anything started in the next 90 seconds is made twice as
  fast), a share given by an ally, a foreign investor (200 gold), a bumper year (6 of a raw
  material).
- **Bad:** thieves (15% of gold), a bribery fine (10% of gold), floods (half of the largest raw
  material), damp in the warehouse (half of the most valuable goods), machines breaking down
  (workshops stop for 60 seconds), a swindler (one share lost).

## World events

On top of the fixed timetable below, each game picks a few world events at random (three in
a 10-minute game, four in 15, five in 20) and fires them at times nobody can plan for:
a hard winter, a drought, forest fires, a new invention, a new fashion, a stock exchange
panic or rally, a company scandal, a crash, a takeover bid, a trade fair, an extra payout to
shareholders, a world tax on the richest country, and relief
for the poorest. They move prices, share prices and what countries hold.

## What happens during the game

Roughly in this order, with a little variation in the timing:

1. **The secret.** The industrial country is told privately that a batch of goods set with
   one piece of amber sells for four times the price. The countries holding amber are not.
2. **A discovery.** A large deposit of a raw material is found in one of the poorer countries.
3. **A price crash.** The most valuable product halves in price.
4. **A strike.** One country's workshops stop for 45 seconds.
5. **Aid.** The poorest country without a workshop is offered a free workshop and mill. If it
   accepts, the lenders keep 30% of everything it sells to the market from then on.
6. **A tariff.** For a while the market pays 30% less for goods from countries that started
   without a workshop.
7. **A boom.** The cheapest product jumps in price.

The raid warning and the invasion come around the middle of the game.

## Winning

A country's worth is its gold, its goods at their usual price, its raw materials at the
market's low price, its buildings at cost, and its shares at their current price. **The
winner is the country whose worth grew the most from where it started**, so every kind of
start is worth playing. The richest country is named too.

During the game each player sees their worth, what it is made of, and where their growth has
come from. When the market closes, the results show for every country:

- what it started with, its worth at the start and the end, and a chart of it;
- where its growth came from: making and selling goods, deals with other countries, share
  payouts, share prices and events, luck included (these always add up to the total);
- what its worth is made of at the start and the end: gold, raw materials, goods, buildings
  and shares;
- how the price of raw materials moved, every deal in order, and four questions to talk about.

## Not yet tested with people

The numbers (prices, production times, building costs, event sizes) are a first guess and
live at the top of `src/game/trade.ts`. In particular it is not yet known whether the poorer
starts can realistically out-grow the industrial one, whether paying out twice a share's price
per game, with ten shares a company, makes the stock exchange too strong compared with making and selling goods.

## On screen

Every section (your country, payday, the stock exchange, making, the market, deals, talk,
news, and each part of the results) can be folded away by clicking its heading. Each device
remembers which sections were folded.
